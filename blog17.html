Spring Into Calm:
Managing Test Anxiety Naturally

Small moments. Big changes.

March brings longer days, fresh blooms, and a sense of renewal — but for many, it also brings the pressure of exams, deadlines, and transitions. Whether you’re a student preparing for tests, a professional facing certifications, or a parent juggling it all, anxiety can sneak in just as easily as spring sunshine.

At Better With IJ Wellness Practice, we believe wellness isn’t just physical — it’s deeply mental and emotional. This month, we’re shining light on stress management, test-taking anxiety, and the healing power of sunlight and mindfulness.

Why Sunlight Matters for Mental Health

It’s no coincidence that brighter days often bring brighter moods. Exposure to natural sunlight increases serotonin, the “feel-good” hormone that helps stabilize mood and improve focus.

Here’s how you can use sunlight intentionally:

  • Take a 10-minute morning walk to boost alertness and concentration.
  • Open your blinds during study or work hours.
  • Eat lunch outdoors at least twice a week.
  • Combine sunlight with gentle movement — yoga, stretching, or breathing exercises.

Just 15–30 minutes of natural light daily can improve energy and sleep quality — two essentials for managing stress.

Breathe Through the Pressure

Test-taking anxiety isn’t about intelligence — it’s about the body’s stress response. When adrenaline surges, it’s your body’s way of saying, “I care about this.” The key is learning how to calm that response before it escalates.

Try this 3-step breathing technique:

  • Inhale deeply for 4 seconds.
  • Hold your breath for 4 seconds.
  • Exhale slowly for 6 seconds.

Repeat this cycle three times before an exam, meeting, or any stressful moment to signal calm and focus.

Mindfulness for Students & Professionals

Mindfulness isn’t complicated. It’s simply the act of being fully present. Here are small ways to integrate calm into your day:

  • Start your morning with 5 minutes of quiet reflection or gratitude.
  • Write down one worry — then one thing you can control.
  • Take study breaks every 45–60 minutes to stretch or hydrate.
  • Practice positive self-talk: replace “I can’t” with “I’m preparing.”

Over time, these micro-habits build resilience and shift your stress patterns.
